03 — Approach

A groma fixes
two lines.

Everything else is measured from them. Three stages, in a fixed order, each closing with a written record you can hand to a board or auditor.

Keep scrolling to traverse

Stage 01

Setting out

Establish what the system is and what it must protect before any control is discussed.

We document the architecture as built rather than as presented: identity providers, network boundaries, integration hubs, data stores and the trust relationships between them. Critical assets are named with their owners, and the assumptions the business relies on are written down so they can be tested rather than inherited.

Your outcome

A single, agreed picture of the estate and the assets that matter.

01

Stage 02

The threat model

Trace how an adversary would move through that picture, and rank what you find.

Threats are modelled against the real architecture, not a generic catalogue. Each credible path is followed from entry to foothold to objective, assessed for likelihood and blast radius, and mapped to the specific control that would break it. The result is a short, defensible list rather than a long one.

Your outcome

Documented attack paths and control priorities your engineers can schedule.

02

Stage 03

The evidence standard

A control only counts when it has been exercised and observed.

Detections are tested against the modelled techniques. Required logs are confirmed to reach the SOC or SIEM with the fields needed to investigate, and gaps are recorded as findings rather than assumptions. Results are written for a board or auditor to read without translation.

Your outcome

Verifiable proof of which controls hold, which do not, and what closes the difference.
